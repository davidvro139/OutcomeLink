## What it is for

OutcomeLink helps a career and technical college **prove how its graduates do**. Accrediting bodies such as the Council on Occupational Education (COE) judge a college largely on three yearly rates for each program:

- **Completion** — of the students who finished or left, how many completed.
- **Placement** — of the graduates who were available, how many found work related to their training (or went on to further study or military service).
- **Licensure** — of the graduates of programs that require a license, how many passed their exam.

Colleges already keep the records behind these numbers — students, enrollments, jobs, licensing results, follow-up calls — but usually scattered across spreadsheets and the student information system. OutcomeLink brings them together, works out the rates the way the accreditor defines them, shows staff where a program stands _before_ the report is due, and produces the reports and evidence behind every figure.

## How it was designed, and why

- **One source of truth.** Every screen, report and export reads the same records, so two people can't be looking at two different placement rates.
- **Every number can be explained.** Any figure can be followed down to the individual students behind it, and each student has a plain-language explanation of why they do or don't count ("How This Student Counts").
- **The rules live in the accreditation engine, not in the screens.** COE's definitions are kept in one place, versioned by rule set, so a rule change is a data change and another accreditor's rules can be added later without rewriting the application.
- **Nothing is silently thrown away.** A row that fails an import is listed with the reason; a data problem becomes a validation issue someone has to resolve or explain.
- **Work is guided, not just recorded.** The tool tells you what needs attention — graduates with no recorded outcome, programs at risk of missing a benchmark, a period that isn't ready to finalize — rather than leaving you to hunt for it.
- **Access follows responsibility.** People see and change only what their role and their assigned programs and campuses allow, and every change is recorded.

## What it is not

- It **does not submit anything to COE** for you. It produces the numbers and evidence you use when you complete the accreditor's own annual report.
- It **does not replace your student information system.** It imports from it and keeps the outcome records the SIS doesn't.
- It **takes no backups of itself.** Backups are the responsibility of whoever runs the server; the app can show whether they are happening (Settings → Backups).
- It is **not certified** by any accreditor or by a FERPA compliance program. The Security page says exactly what it does and doesn't do.
- It **doesn't make decisions.** Statuses such as "At risk" are aids to judgment based on the last computed results, not findings.
